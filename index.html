Key pillars

Not an antibiotic

A lysin is an enzyme that viruses use to burst bacteria. It grips the surface of a staph germ and cuts its wall from outside, so germs that already resist antibiotics are still killed.

No resistance seen

In the laboratory, staph was regrown a hundred times in the presence of the enzyme and never learned to survive it. Mupirocin failed in the same experiment every time.

Staph, and only staph

The enzyme recognises a shape found on staph. It has no effect on acne bacteria or Gram-negative organisms, so the rest of the skin’s community stays in place.

Through the slime shield

Staph hides under a slimy layer called biofilm. In laboratory tests the lysin cleared biofilms that vancomycin and linezolid could not.

Infected artificial joints: built to treat, not only prevent

Our most advanced programme, LYSG101, led with our parent group, Precisio Biotix, in the US.

01

Prevents and treats

Designed both to stop a joint infection before it starts and to clear one that has already taken hold.

02

No surgery to give it

The enzyme does not need an operation to be given, unlike approaches that can only be used during surgery.

03

Early patient results

Seven patients with no options left were approved for treatment under special US FDA permission. In the clearest case, an eight-year resistant infection cleared within two days and stayed clear for over 100 days. Not every patient has responded.

04

Aiming for a lasting answer

The goal is to clear the infection, not only manage it. Many patients today face repeat surgery, lifelong antibiotics or amputation.

LYSG101 is investigational and not approved for sale in any country. Results from individual patients do not predict the outcome of clinical trials.
